# Post 02: The Incentive Mismatch — They're Not Your Enemy

**Phase:** 1 — Establish the Problem
**Status:** Draft
**Target:** Week 1, Post 2

---

Infrastructure teams aren't blocking your security work because they don't care about security.

They're blocking it because they get punished for outages and not rewarded for security posture. If a change they make breaks production, that's on them. If a vulnerability they didn't patch gets exploited six months later, that's a security problem.

The incentives are asymmetric. Of course they avoid risky changes.

I've watched this dynamic play out across four organizations. The security team files a request. Infrastructure puts it in the backlog. It sits. An incident happens. Everyone wonders why the work wasn't done. The security team gets frustrated. The infrastructure team feels blamed for something they were never actually set up to prioritize.

Nobody wins. The cycle repeats.

The CISOs I've watched struggle the longest are the ones who treated this as a motivation problem — if only they could convince the other team to care more. The ones who made real progress treated it as a design problem. If the current system produces this outcome, what would a different system look like?

The answer isn't more persuasion. It's removing the dependency entirely — giving security teams a way to execute changes that's safe enough, governed enough, and reversible enough that infrastructure teams don't need to be in the critical path for every change.

They're not your enemy. They're rational actors responding to how they're measured.

Design around that. Don't fight it.
