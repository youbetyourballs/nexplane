Control plane for infrastructure change
Infrastructure changes are inevitable. Outages are optional.
Plan, approve, execute, and roll back infrastructure changes across cloud, network, identity, Kubernetes, endpoints, and security systems — from one control plane.
Nexplane is the control plane for infrastructure change.

Strongest audience fit
Platform engineers, SREs, infrastructure leads who own reliability
What it emphasizes
Reliability and control. The headline is memorable, punchy, and resonates with anyone who has survived an outage caused by a change. Clear platform metaphor (control plane).
Potential risk / weakness
"Control plane" is Kubernetes jargon — may not land for network or security engineers. "Outages are optional" could read as overpromising to a skeptical VP.
V2 · Category view →
A new category of infrastructure tool
The first control plane built for infrastructure change.
Not ticketing. Not provisioning. Not another automation tool. Nexplane gives infrastructure teams one place to understand, simulate, approve, execute, roll back, and remember every change.
Nexplane owns the change — from planning through rollback and institutional memory.
Request demo Read docs →
Strongest audience fit
VP Engineering, CTO, infrastructure architects evaluating tooling categories
What it emphasizes
Category creation. "Not X, not Y" pattern positions against existing tools rather than competing with them. Appeals to buyers frustrated with their current stack.
Potential risk / weakness
Category creation is a longer sales motion. "First" is a bold claim that invites skepticism. Requires more education for buyers who don't yet feel the gap.
V3 · Flywheel view →
Infrastructure that compounds over time
Every change makes the next change safer.
Nexplane turns infrastructure changes into institutional knowledge — linking assets, approvals, dependencies, simulations, executions, rollbacks, and outcomes into a compounding system of record.
Infrastructure that learns from every change.
Request demo Read docs →
Strongest audience fit
Engineering leaders who've experienced knowledge drain from turnover, or teams rebuilding after incidents
What it emphasizes
Compounding value and institutional knowledge. Unique positioning angle — no other tool in this space claims to get better over time. Appeals to longer-term buyers.
Potential risk / weakness
The flywheel is abstract — harder to grasp than "outages are optional." Needs more explanation. Less useful for buyers evaluating immediate pain.

Built for humans and AI agents

Nexplane gives humans, Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor, Codex, and internal agents the same governed interface to understand infrastructure, simulate changes, request approvals, execute safely, and roll back.

Strongest audience fit
CTOs and architects at AI-forward teams already using Cursor, Claude, or internal agents; anyone with an AI mandate
What it emphasizes
AI/agent readiness. Captures "AI budget unlock" question. MCP server is a real differentiator. Timely — AI ops is top of mind for engineering leadership.
Potential risk / weakness
Risks being perceived as an AI tool rather than infrastructure tooling. Could alienate ops-focused buyers who aren't yet running agents. "Agent era" may age poorly.
V5 · Operational Risk view →
Operational risk reduction
Eliminate uncertainty before infrastructure change.
Nexplane helps infrastructure and operations leaders reduce failed changes, shorten maintenance windows, preserve institutional knowledge, and prove every change was approved, validated, and reversible.
Less risk. More confidence. Every change.
Request demo Read docs →
Strongest audience fit
Operations leads, compliance-adjacent teams, CTOs at regulated industries (financial services, healthcare, government)
What it emphasizes
Risk reduction and auditability. Speaks to buyers with real compliance and change management pressure. "Prove every change was approved" is a direct compliance hook.
Potential risk / weakness
Sounds like change management/ITSM tooling (ServiceNow territory). Risk-reduction framing can attract buyers who want governance theater, not real infrastructure tooling.
